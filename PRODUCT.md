# Product

## Register

product

## Users

PharmIQ serves Australian independent pharmacy owners, dispensary managers, and multi-site pharmacy operators. They work in high-interruption pharmacy environments where expiry risk, stock movement, markdown decisions, and operational exceptions need to be understood quickly across desktop dashboards and handheld pharmacy PDT devices.

Users are trying to track and manage expiry and stock workflows without adding administrative drag. They need fast scan capture, reliable offline behaviour, clear product lookup, actionable alerts, and reporting that helps protect margins before stock becomes waste.

## Product Purpose

PharmIQ is a pharmacy operations command centre focused on expiry management, inventory visibility, markdown workflows, reporting, subscription-managed access, and handheld PWA scanning. It exists to turn pharmacy stock data into timely operational action: reduce expiry write-offs, recover value from slow-moving products, maintain confidence across store areas, and support multi-site decision-making.

Success looks like a pharmacist or owner seeing the next action immediately: what is expiring, where it is located, what needs attention, what has synced, and what business impact is at risk.

## Brand Personality

PharmIQ is authoritative, calm, precise, and commercially grounded. The product should feel like expert infrastructure for pharmacy operators: dependable under pressure, respectful of clinical dignity, and direct enough for use behind the dispensary counter.

The voice is confident and plain-spoken. UI copy should be short, specific, and action-oriented. Avoid generic analytics language when a concrete pharmacy outcome can be named.

## Anti-references

Do not make the app feel like generic SaaS, sterile corporate healthcare software, playful consumer gamification, or a decorative dashboard built around vanity metrics. Avoid ornamental amber, excessive teal, glassmorphism, gradient text, identical card grids, nested cards, and marketing-style hero layouts inside authenticated workflows.

Avoid UI patterns that slow pharmacy work: unclear scan states, hidden sync failures, small handheld tap targets, vague error messages, decorative motion, and invented controls where standard tables, forms, tabs, filters, and navigation would be faster.

The detailed brand system, visual restrictions, and competitive positioning live in `brand-identity/brand-guidelines.md` and are surfaced for design tooling through `DESIGN.md`.

## Design Principles

1. Clarity before chrome: the next operational action should be visible faster than the visual treatment.
2. Respect pharmacy pressure: layouts, labels, and states must survive interruptions, handheld use, and partial connectivity.
3. Treat urgency as functional: amber, red, and warning language appear only when the user truly needs to act.
4. Make data feel accountable: counts, expiry windows, sync state, and reports should be specific, traceable, and easy to compare.
5. Maintain clinical dignity: use restrained product patterns, precise typography, and familiar controls instead of decorative novelty.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Default to WCAG AA or better for text and interactive states. Preserve large handheld touch targets, especially for scanning workflows and pharmacy PDT devices. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus states, screen-reader-friendly status text, reduced-motion preferences, and colour-independent state communication.

Do not rely on low-contrast muted text for operational information. Warning and expiry states must be conveyed through label, icon, structure, or copy as well as colour.
